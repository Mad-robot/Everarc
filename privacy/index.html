Privacy policy

Last updated 8 November 2026. The same policy is in the app, under Settings → Privacy policy.

The short version

Everarc has no account, no server for your data, no analytics and no ads. Everything you enter stays on your phone, encrypted.

What is stored, and where

Your routines, their history, notes, settings and progress photos are stored only on your phone. The database is encrypted with a key kept in the phone's secure storage; photos are encrypted files.

Everarc keeps its data out of iCloud and Google backups, unless you turn on Back up automatically: then one password-encrypted file rides along in the phone's own backup, and on iPhone (and in an Android phone-to-phone transfer) the photo files too, still sealed with a key that only that password-encrypted file holds. Otherwise the only copy that can leave the phone is a backup file you make yourself, encrypted with a password you choose. Where you save it is up to you.

Photo checks (blur, light, how your head is turned compared with your first photo, and the plain-background view) run on the phone. No face data is stored, apart from the photos themselves.

What leaves the phone

Nothing about your routines or photos. Everarc sends no usage data, crash reports or identifiers anywhere.

Buying Plus goes through the App Store or Google Play; Apple or Google handle the payment under their own privacy policies, and Everarc only learns whether you have Plus.

On Android, activating a licence key (if you have one) sends the key and an anonymous device code, which can't be traced back to you or your phone outside Everarc, to the activation service once.

A phone with its own AI (Apple Intelligence or Gemini Nano) gets its model from Apple or Google. If yours has none and you choose to download a model in Settings, Everarc fetches it once from Hugging Face; nothing about you is sent with the request. Every model runs on the phone, and your data is never sent to it over the internet.

If you turn on a Health switch in Settings (Apple Health or Health Connect), Everarc reads only that type (steps, or the weight on a photo day) when it needs it and never stores what it reads. It saves only your mood, mindful minutes and water there, from what you log.

Siri and Shortcuts can see the names of today's open routines (never private ones, and none with the app lock on) so you can tick them by voice.

Wear OS, only if you turn it on: today's routine names, times and steps go to your watch, and your ticks come back, through Google Play services, which may carry them through Google's servers when Bluetooth is off. Private routines show as "A routine"; with the app lock on, nothing is sent unless you allow names on the watch.

Moving to a new phone happens directly between the two phones on your own Wi-Fi. Nothing goes to the internet.

Permissions

Notifications, to remind you. Camera and photos, only for photo routines, reading a medicine label, and scanning the code when moving to a new phone, and only when you take or pick a photo. Face ID or fingerprint, only if you turn on the app lock. You can turn each off in the phone's settings; the rest of the app keeps working.

Calendar, only if you allow it: Everarc reads when you're busy (never event names or details) for "Nudge when I'm free", and writes only to its own "Everarc" calendar for routines you choose to show there. Local network, only to move to a new phone.

Deleting your data

Deleting a routine or a photo removes it from your phone. Deleting the app removes everything Everarc stored. Backup files you saved elsewhere are yours to delete. Because Everarc has no server and no account, there is nothing held anywhere else to delete.

Children

Everarc collects nothing from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version ships in an app update and is published here with its date.
